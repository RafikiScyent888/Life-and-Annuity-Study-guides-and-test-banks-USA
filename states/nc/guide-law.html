North Carolina law: life

Test part F

The Commissioner and licensing

  • The Commissioner of Insurance is elected by the people for four years. If the office becomes vacant, the Governor fills it for the rest of the term (G.S. 58-2-5).
  • A resident producer must be at least 18. The Commissioner can issue a temporary producer license, without an exam, for up to 180 days, for example after a producer dies or is called to military service. A nonresident producer is never required to have a resident producer countersign (Article 33).
  • Continuing education: up to 24 credit hours every 2 years. A producer who misses the compliance date has 4 months to finish, with a $75 reinstatement fee, before the license goes inactive. Nonresident producers have no NC CE requirement (NCDOI).

Selling life insurance and annuities

  • Before a sales presentation, the agent must say they're a life insurance agent and name the insurer (G.S. 58-60-20).
  • The buyer gets the Buyer's Guide and Policy Summary before the insurer accepts the first premium, or with the policy if it has an unconditional refund provision of at least 10 days. The summary is titled “Statement of Policy Cost and Benefit Information.” Failing to deliver them counts as misrepresentation (Article 60).
  • Annuities: the disclosure document and buyer's guide come at or before application, or within 5 business days for other sales. If they weren't given at application, the buyer gets a free look of at least 15 days (G.S. 58-60-135).
  • Replacement: the producer presents and reads the Notice Regarding Replacement no later than when the application is taken. The applicant and producer both sign it, and a copy is left with the applicant (11 NCAC 12 .0605). Replacement includes lapsing, surrendering or converting an existing policy to reduced paid-up or extended term, or using it in a financed purchase (.0602).
Watch outNC law has no general free-look period for individual life policies. The 10 days is the refund provision an insurer needs if it wants to deliver the Buyer's Guide with the policy. Annuities have the 15-day rule above.

Unfair practices, fraud and privacy

  • Rebating (anything of value not in the policy, offered to induce a sale) is an unfair trade practice. Policy dividends on participating policies are not rebates (G.S. 58-63-15).
  • Unauthorized insurers: representing one unknowingly is a Class 1 misdemeanor; if the producer knew or should have known, it's a Class H felony. Either way the producer is strictly liable for unpaid claims (G.S. 58-33-95).
  • A producer who knowingly makes a false statement on an application, or to obtain a commission, commits a Class 1 misdemeanor (G.S. 58-33-105). A false statement to collect a claim is a Class H felony under $100,000 and a Class C felony at $100,000 or more (G.S. 58-2-161).
  • Privacy: pretext interviews are banned except in narrow claim-fraud cases. An authorization to collect information for a life application is valid for up to 30 months. After an adverse underwriting decision, the insurer gives specific reasons within 21 business days of a written request (Article 39).

North Carolina law: health

Test part G

The licensing, unfair practices, fraud and privacy rules in the life section apply to health producers too. These are the health-specific rules.

NC's version of the uniform provisions is in G.S. 58-51-15.

Who's covered

  • An individual or family hospitalization policy must let the buyer return it within 10 days for a full refund (G.S. 58-51-10).
  • Newborns are covered from the moment of birth. If adding the child costs extra, enroll within 30 days of birth. Adopted and foster children are covered from placement on the same 30-day terms (G.S. 58-51-30).
  • A dependent child whose disability prevents self-supporting work stays covered past the age limit. Proof is due within 31 days of reaching that age, then no more than once a year (G.S. 58-51-25).
  • Policies must cover a screening mammogram every year from age 50, every other year at 40–49, and one baseline at 35–39, plus cervical cancer screening (G.S. 58-51-57).
  • Long-term care premiums can rise at most 25% for a policyholder in one calendar year (G.S. 58-51-95).
